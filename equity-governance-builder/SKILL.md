## Governance Architecture

### The Three-Body Problem of Employee Ownership

Any employee-owned company must resolve three tensions:
1. **Scale vs participation**: 90,000 people cannot vote on every decision.
2. **Democracy vs efficiency**: Employee democracy must not paralyze operations.
3. **Founder vision vs collective will**: The founder's strategic insight must be preserved without becoming autocracy.

Huawei's solution: **Representative democracy + institutionalized veto + rotating leadership.**

### Layer 1: The Shareholding Platform (持股平台)

**Legal form**: Huawei uses the " Huawei Investment Holdings Trade Union Committee" (华为投资控股有限公司工会委员会) as the registered shareholder holding 98.99% of shares. Individual employees are NOT direct company shareholders.

**Why a platform is necessary**:
- China Company Law limits LLC shareholders to 50; non-listed股份公司 to 200 founders.
- Direct registration of 90,000 employees is legally impossible.
- Centralized platform enables unified dividend distribution, repurchase, and record-keeping.

**Platform governance rules**:
1. **Separate from trade union functions**: The shareholding platform and the recreational/social trade union must be legally and financially independent.
   - Trade union: Organizes basketball games, handles welfare complaints. Funded by company payroll contribution (2% of wages).
   - Shareholding platform: Manages employee capital, votes on corporate governance. Funded by employee share purchases.
2. **Employee Representative Meeting (持股员工代表会)**: The supreme authority of the shareholding platform.
   - Elected by all employee-shareholders on a **1 share = 1 vote** basis.
   - Huawei has ~115 representatives for 90,000+ shareholders.
   - Term: Typically 3-5 years.
   - Powers: Elect board members, approve major capital changes, amend governance charter, approve dividend policy.
3. **Platform committee**: Day-to-day management of share registry, dividend calculation, repurchase execution.

### Layer 2: The Company Board (董事会)

**Election**: Board members are nominated by the employee representative meeting and elected from among employee-shareholders.

**Huawei's board structure (2015 example)**:
- 17 board members
- Includes chairman, deputy chairmen, executive directors, and ordinary directors
- **Key feature**: Founder任正非 is one of several deputy chairmen, NOT chairman.

**Board committees**:
- Human Resources Committee
- Finance Committee
- Strategy and Development Committee
- Audit Committee

**Decision scope**: Strategic direction, major investments, executive appointments, M&A.

### Layer 3: The Rotating CEO (轮值CEO)

**Mechanism**: Three deputy chairmen rotate as acting CEO for 6-month terms.

**Powers during rotation**:
- Full operational authority as "highest responsible person for corporate management and crisis management."
- Accountable to the board.

**Rationale**:
- Prevents any single leader from accumulating excessive power.
- Tests multiple leaders in the top role before permanent selection.
- Maintains strategic continuity while refreshing execution energy.

### Layer 4: Founder Veto (否决权)

**Scope**: Limited veto on major governance matters only:
- Capital increase / structural change
- Governance charter amendments
- Board and supervisor candidate nominations

**Constraints**:
- Veto, not decision power. 任正非 cannot approve unilaterally; he can only block.
- Used rarely. "轻易不会使用."
- Real influence comes through "management philosophy and thought leadership" — frequent internal speeches that shape culture.

## Governance Charter Design

A governance charter (治理章程) is essential. Huawei spent **3 years** developing theirs, studying:
- US Constitution (stability: 27 amendments in 200+ years)
- Ancient Chinese political philosophy
- Western corporate governance best practices
- Huawei's own 30-year operational experience

### Charter must specify:

| Element | Specification |
|---------|--------------|
| Shareholder rights | Who can vote, on what, how votes are counted |
| Representative election | Eligibility, nomination, campaign rules, term limits |
| Board composition | Size, committees, qualification, removal |
| CEO selection | Rotation rules, emergency succession, evaluation |
| Founder role | Explicit scope of veto and advisory powers |
| Supervisor independence | How supervisors are selected and protected from interference |
| Amendment rules | Supermajority requirements to prevent casual changes |
| Dispute resolution | Internal arbitration → external arbitration → litigation |

## Implementation for Different Scales

### < 50 Employees
- **Structure**: Direct shareholder registration (LLC form).
- **Governance**: Simple shareholder meeting + board of 3-5.
- **Founder role**: Can hold majority voting rights temporarily, but document the sunset condition (e.g., "reduces to 30% at 100 employees").

### 50-200 Employees
- **Structure**: Single limited partnership as holding platform.
- **Governance**: GP (founder/trusted entity) manages platform; LPs elect advisory council.
- **Founder role**: GP retains operational control, but LP agreement specifies profit distribution and major decision rights.

### 200-2,000 Employees
- **Structure**: Multiple parallel partnerships or early union platform.
- **Governance**: Elected employee council (50-100 reps) with real voting power on board nominations and charter changes.
- **Founder role**: Board member with veto on 3-5 specified matters. Operational authority delegated to professional management.

### 2,000+ Employees
- **Structure**: Union platform or multi-layer partnership with top-hold entity.
- **Governance**: Full representative democracy — employee rep meeting as supreme authority; rotating CEO; independent audit committee.
- **Founder role**: Honorary/advisory position with limited veto. Real power shifts to institutional design.

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Risk | Fix |
|--------------|------|-----|
| Founder holds 51%+ directly | Employee ownership is cosmetic; no real democracy | Cap founder at <20% before 100 employees |
| Employee reps are appointed, not elected | Representative democracy becomes rubber stamp | Mandate secret-ballot elections with contested races |
| Shareholding platform funds mixed with union funds | Legal liability, tax disaster, loss of employee trust | Separate bank accounts, auditors, decision bodies |
| No written governance charter | Founder can change rules arbitrarily; disputes escalate | Write charter early; require supermajority to amend |
| Board dominated by founder's family/friends | Independent oversight destroyed | Reserve 1/3+ board seats for independent employee reps |

## STOP Checkpoint

- [ ] Can employee representatives be elected without founder interference?
- [ ] Is there a written charter that limits founder power and specifies its own amendment process?
- [ ] Are shareholding platform assets legally segregated from trade union / company assets?
- [ ] Do employees understand their voting rights and exercise them?
- [ ] Is there an independent audit mechanism for share registry and dividend calculations?

If any answer is NO, governance legitimacy is at risk.
