# Individual Development Planner

Design and execute Individual Development Plans (IDP) that connect organizational needs with personal growth, using the 70-20-10 principle, manager feedback, and structured mentorship — not as annual paperwork, but as a living contract.

## When to Use

When the user needs to:
- Create an IDP system for high-potential or key-role employees
- Move development planning from HR-mandated forms to genuinely useful growth roadmaps
- Integrate IDP with mentor assignment, manager feedback, and quarterly review cycles
- Design Manager Feedback Programs (MFP) that help leaders see themselves as their team sees them
- Ensure development activities actually build capability, not just check boxes

## Core Concepts

**IDP = Roadmap**: A 1–3 year plan that maps an employee's strengths, gaps, aspirations, and development activities against organizational needs.

**70-20-10 Rule**:
- **70%** learning from challenging jobs, stretch assignments, and on-the-job experiences
- **20%** learning from mentors, peers, and feedback relationships
- **10%** learning from formal courses, books, and training

**MFP (Manager Feedback Program)**: A structured, subordinate-to-manager feedback process that measures leadership behaviors and generates actionable improvement plans.

**Three-Party Contract**: Employee (owns the plan), Manager (provides resources, feedback, and coaching), HR/Training (supplies tools, tracks progress, resolves blockers).

## Operating Procedure

### Step 1: Launch IDP with Diagnosis

Employee completes a self-assessment covering:
- **Capability gaps**: Current abilities vs. role requirements vs. future aspirations
- **Strengths to amplify**: Areas where further investment yields outsized returns
- **Career goals**: 1-year and 3-year targets (role level, function, geography)

Manager independently assesses the same dimensions. Then they meet to:
1. Compare perceptions; discuss gaps in self-view vs. manager-view
2. Agree on 3–5 priority development areas (not more — focus is essential)
3. Link each area to a concrete organizational need (why developing this matters to the business)

**Rule**: IDP is about capabilities and experiences, not routine work tasks. Do not confuse development goals with quarterly KPIs.

### Step 2: Design Development Activities (70-20-10)

For each priority area, select activities across the 70-20-10 mix:

| Type | Examples | Ownership |
|------|----------|-----------|
| **70 — Experience** | Lead a cross-functional project; turn around a failing account; launch in a new market; take a rotational assignment | Manager assigns or sponsors |
| **20 — Exposure** | Monthly mentor meetings; peer coaching circle; shadowing a senior leader; presenting to executive committee | HR arranges; employee drives |
| **10 — Education** | Online course; certification; book study with reflection paper; external workshop | Employee selects; manager approves budget |

**Guidelines**:
- Every IDP must include at least one **stretch assignment** (70%) — otherwise it is just a training wishlist
- For senior leaders, add a **community contribution** activity (e.g., mentor a junior high-potential, write a case study)
- Match activity difficulty to readiness: don’t send a novice to negotiate a merger alone

### Step 3: Set Measurable Milestones

For each activity, define:
- **Success indicator**: What does "done well" look like? (Specific, observable)
- **Evidence**: What artifact demonstrates completion? (presentation, project report, certification, 360 feedback)
- **Deadline**: End date or milestone checkpoints
- **Support needed**: Budget, manager time, mentor access, training enrollment

Example:
> **Gap**: Strategic thinking
> **Activity**: Draft a 3-year market entry plan for Region X (70%)
> **Measure**: Plan reviewed and approved by regional president
> **Evidence**: Written plan + presentation recording
> **Deadline**: 6 months
> **Support**: 4 hours/month coaching from Strategy VP; access to market data

### Step 4: Run MFP in Parallel

For managers of IDP holders, run the Manager Feedback Program every 6–12 months:

1. **Survey**: Direct reports complete a confidential 10–15 question survey on the manager’s people-management behaviors (trust, feedback, development support, values modeling, etc.)
2. **Report generation**: Aggregate results into a feedback report showing strengths and gaps
3. **Feedback meeting**: Manager holds a face-to-face meeting with the team to discuss findings, acknowledge gaps, and commit to 2–3 specific improvements
4. **Improvement plan**: Manager writes a personal improvement plan with timelines; shares with team and skip-level leader

**Critical success factors**:
- Anonymity must be protected (minimum 4 respondents)
- Manager must not retaliate or guess who said what
- Follow-through is tracked; no action = loss of credibility

### Step 5: Execute with Quarterly Reviews

Every quarter, employee and manager hold a 30-minute IDP review:
- Progress against each activity
- Barriers encountered and support needed
- Recalibration if business priorities shift
- Recognition of completed milestones

HR tracks:
- IDP completion rates by population
- Correlation between IDP completion and promotion/retention rates
- Common barriers (e.g., "no time from manager") that suggest systemic issues

### Step 6: Close the Cycle and Renew

At year-end:
- Employee self-evaluates IDP achievement
- Manager evaluates and provides qualitative feedback
- HR records outcomes for talent review and succession planning
- New IDP drafted for next year, incorporating lessons learned

**Recognition**: Award "Best Development Journey" or similar to employees who completed ambitious IDPs and visibly grew. Publicize their stories.

## Anti-Patterns

- **HR-only ownership**: IDP becomes a form employees fill out because HR requires it, with no manager engagement.
- **Training-only IDP**: Every development activity is a course. Ignores the 70% experiential learning.
- **Vague goals**: "Improve communication" without defining what that means or how it will be measured.
- **No follow-through**: IDP is written in January and never discussed again until December.
- **Punitive MFP**: Using feedback to punish low-scoring managers instead of developing them. Destroys honesty.
