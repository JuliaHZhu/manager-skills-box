# supply-chain-triad-collaborator

Align R&D, Sales, and Supply Chain into a unified customer-facing force using the Iron Triangle (AR/SR/FR) operating model, eliminating the "department wall" problem.

## When to Use

- The user complains that sales forecasts are always wrong and supply chain gets blamed
- The user says R&D designs products that are hard to manufacture or deliver
- The user describes internal friction between sales, R&D, and operations/supply chain
- The user wants to build customer-facing project teams instead of siloed functional responders
- The user mentions "iron triangle", 铁三角, or asks how Huawei broke departmental silos in customer delivery

## Core Principle

**A supply chain operating in isolation is meaningless.** Supply chain management is not just vendor management; it is the process of integrating external resources and breaking internal walls to efficiently satisfy customer demand.

The competition of the future is not company vs. company, but **supply chain vs. supply chain**.

## The Triad: Three Coordinated Functions

| Function | Role | Typical Department |
|----------|------|-------------------|
| **AR** (Account Responsibility) | Customer relationship manager, commercial owner, revenue accountable | Sales / Account Manager |
| **SR** (Solution Responsibility) | Technical solution design, product configuration, feasibility | R&D / Solution Manager |
| **FR** (Fulfillment Responsibility) | Delivery execution, supply chain operations, project logistics | Supply Chain / Delivery Manager |

All three face the customer together, share accountability for customer satisfaction, and jointly solve problems.

## Origin: The Sudan Telecom Lesson (2006)

Huawei lost a major bid in Sudan because:
- Customer relationships were not strong enough (AR gap)
- Solutions did not fully match customer needs (SR gap)
- Delivery quality and timeliness failed (FR gap)
- Department walls caused internal friction and slow response

The fix: create a unified project core team (AR + SR + FR) for each key customer/project, consolidating all customer interfaces into one face.

Result: won the next Senegal project in 2007; model rolled out company-wide.

## Implementation Guidelines

### 1. Customer Demand as the Starting Point

Every supply chain action must originate from validated customer demand, not from internal convenience or functional habit.

- Sales brings real customer requirements and commitments
- R&D confirms technical feasibility and translates needs into configurable solutions
- Supply Chain confirms delivery feasibility, lead times, and cost before promising

### 2. Joint Ownership of Customer Commitments

The triad co-signs delivery commitments to the customer. No single function can unilaterally promise delivery dates, technical specs, or commercial terms.

### 3. Co-Located or Tightly Coupled Collaboration

- Physical co-location for strategic accounts when possible
- Shared KPIs tied to customer satisfaction and project profitability, not just functional metrics
- Regular triad stand-ups (daily/weekly) during active projects

### 4. Unified Escalation and Issue Resolution

- When problems arise (quality defect, component shortage, design change), the triad meets first before escalating separately
- Supply Chain brings visibility of constraints; R&D brings design flexibility; Sales brings customer negotiation leverage

### 5. Map Supply Chain into the Iron Triangle

In Huawei's model, the FR (Fulfillment Responsibility) role is explicitly the Supply Chain / Delivery Manager. This elevates supply chain from a back-office support function to a front-line competitive weapon.

## Warning Signs of Silo Failure

- Sales promises delivery dates without consulting operations
- R&D releases designs without considering supplier capability or logistics constraints
- Supply Chain optimizes inventory or cost without understanding customer value or sales urgency
- Each function blames the others when customer complaints arise
- "胶片文化" (slide-deck culture) where internal presentations matter more than actual customer outcomes

## Key Rules

1. **Customer demand is the trigger** — not production plan, not inventory level, not procurement convenience
2. **Three-way collaboration is mandatory** — R&D + Sales + Supply Chain must align before any customer commitment
3. **FR (Supply Chain) must have a seat at the customer table** — not hidden behind sales

## Boundaries & Anti-Patterns

- **Not for**: single-function optimization (e.g., pure inventory reduction without sales and R&D alignment); internal team building without customer-facing purpose
- The Iron Triangle works best for project-based or account-based businesses; high-volume transactional models may need variant designs
- Do not let the triad become a bureaucracy with excessive coordination meetings; keep it lean and empowered
- If one leg of the triangle is persistently weak (e.g., no capable supply chain talent), fix the capability gap before restructuring collaboration
