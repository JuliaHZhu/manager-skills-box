## Core Methodology

任正非 described management as "twisting a hemp rope" (拧麻花): the tighter you twist, the stronger it gets — even after 4,000 years, like the ropes in Egypt's pyramids. Management systems must be designed with intentional tension among opposing forces.

### Twist Type 1: Front-Back Twist (前后拧)

Add a time dimension to management emphasis. Alternate focus over periods like waves.

1. **Phase A — emphasis on expansion**: Prioritize market share, new products, geographic expansion. Metrics favor growth. Tolerance for inefficiency is higher.
2. **Phase B — emphasis on efficiency**: Prioritize cost reduction, process optimization, quality improvement. Metrics favor profitability and operational excellence.
3. **Rhythm**: Not simultaneous dual focus (which confuses priorities), but sequential emphasis that creates a breathing cycle.
4. **Application**: Annual planning can alternate themes; product lifecycle alternates between feature push and quality consolidation.

### Twist Type 2: Top-Bottom Twist (上下拧)

Align different organizational levels around different but complementary objectives.

1. **Top layer twists toward strategy**:
   - Board / EMT focus on direction, culture,干部 selection, and监督 control.
   - They define "what mountain to climb" and ensure resources are available.

2. **Middle layer twists toward execution**:
   - Department heads translate strategy into operational plans.
   - They optimize资源配置 and cross-functional coordination.

3. **Bottom layer twists toward efficiency and效益**:
   - Frontline teams focus on customer satisfaction, productivity, and quality.
   - They generate the actual revenue and learning.

4. **The twist**: If all levels optimize for the same thing (e.g., everyone focuses on cost-cutting), strategic investment dies. If all focus on strategy, execution falters. The opposing pulls create organizational strength.

### Twist Type 3: Inside-Outside Twist (里外拧)

Balance internal and external stakeholder interests so that protecting one reinforces the others.

1. **Internal twist — shareholder vs. employee**:
   - Shareholders want returns; employees want growth and income.
   - Huawei's ESOP design aligns them: employees are shareholders. The twist becomes self-reinforcing.

2. **External twist — customer vs. partner**:
   - Customers want lower prices and better service; partners want sustainable margins.
   - The twist: long-term partnerships require both sides to profit. Huawei's "win-win" is not charity — it is the rope holding the relationship together.

3. **Combined twist**: Every policy must be checked against both internal and external effects. A policy that benefits employees but hurts customers will eventually hurt employees too (company declines). A policy that squeezes partners for short-term profit eventually destroys the supply base.

### Designing Your Own Twist

Step-by-step:
1. **Identify the opposing forces** in your context (e.g., speed vs. quality, centralization vs. autonomy, standardization vs. customization).
2. **Determine the twist axis**: time (sequential), hierarchy (layered), or stakeholder (balanced).
3. **Set the twist rhythm**: How often do you switch emphasis? How tight is the rope?
4. **Build feedback loops**: Ensure that each force can sense when it is pulling too hard and self-correct.
5. **Monitor rope integrity**: If one strand breaks, the whole rope weakens. Maintain all strands.

### The Scale-Efficiency Paradox Resolution

When scaling, efficiency often drops.拧麻花 resolves this:
- **Without twist**: Scale expands → bureaucracy grows → efficiency dies.
- **With twist**: Scale expands → management system tightens → efficiency is demanded as the counter-force → the rope holds.

任正非: "加强管理制度建设，依靠管理降低成本，向管理要效益，不屈不挠地坚持管理制度改革。"

## STOP Checkpoints

- **STOP if** management emphasizes only one direction without a countervailing force. Single-strand rope snaps under load.
- **STOP if** the twist is so tight that it strangles initiative. Tension must be productive, not suffocating.
- **STOP if** opposing forces are left unmanaged and become open conflict. They must be braided, not fighting.

## Anti-Patterns (Blacklist)

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails | Correct Practice |
|--------------|--------------|------------------|
| Simultaneous dual emphasis | Confuses priorities; resources split ineffectively | Sequential twist: front-back |
| All levels same KPI | Strategic or operational gaps emerge | Layered twist: top-bottom |
| Zero-sum stakeholder management | One side's win is the other's loss; relationships break | Balanced twist: inside-outside |
| Loose rope (no tension) | System drifts; standards erode | Maintain intentional tension |
| Over-tightened rope | Innovation crushed; burnout rises | Productive tension, not suffocation |
