# Org Synergy Architect

Design organizational synergy mechanisms using Huawei's three-layer approach: process integration, matrix structure, and market-driven internal mechanisms. Use when cross-department collaboration fails despite cultural appeals and committee meetings.

## When to Use

Activate when the user describes:
- 部门墙 / 协同困难 / cross-department conflict
- 矩阵组织 / 双计双考 / matrix structure
- 一线呼唤炮火 / 机关资源化 / 资源市场化
- 流程拉通 / IPD / LTC / ITR / process-driven organization
- 前后方协同 / 前方后方打架 / frontline vs back office
- 协同机制不见效 / 委员会没用 / 跨部门项目组貌合神离
- 拧麻花 / 拧在一起

Do NOT activate for: single-department process optimization, team-building activities, or pure HR collaboration training.

## Core Concepts

### The Synergy Problem

Most companies try to solve collaboration through:
1. **Culture** ("协作" in values) — weak, unenforceable
2. **Incentives** (awards/punishments) — superficial
3. **Committees** (cross-functional teams) — no real power or shared interest

These are **surface-level** solutions. Huawei solves synergy at **deep structure**.

### Three Deep Levers

#### 1. Process: From Fragmentation to End-to-End Flow

**Problem**: Functional organizations create chimneys (烟囱) — each department optimizes locally, handoffs break down.

**Huawei solution**: Build Business Operation System (BOS) around IPD/LTC/ITR:
- Process is the rail (铁轨); culture/incentives are the train (火车)
- Process defines who does what, when, to what standard
- Natural handoffs replace negotiated handoffs
- Example: LTC (Lead to Cash) integrates sales, delivery, finance, legal into one flow

**Key principle**: "企业管理的目标是流程化组织建设" — The goal of enterprise management is building process-driven organizations.

**Analogy**: Orchestra needs sheet music (process), not just collaboration culture. Without sheet music, even willing musicians produce noise.

#### 2. Structure: Matrix Twisting (拧麻花)

**Functional structure**: Clean divisions, poor collaboration
**Divisional structure**: Clean accountability, duplicated resources

**Huawei matrix**: Both vertical (function) and horizontal (project/region) managers share the same subordinates.

**Why it works**:
- Shared subordinate = shared fate
- Vertical and horizontal have **common interest** in subordinate's success
- 双计双考 (dual accounting, dual assessment): Project success counts for both vertical and horizontal managers

**Example table**:

| Horizontal \ Vertical | Product Line A | Product Line B | Product Line C | Region Total |
|-----------------------|---------------|---------------|---------------|-------------|
| Region 1 | Target a | Target b | Target c | Region 1 sum |
| Region 2 | Target d | Target e | Target f | Region 2 sum |
| Region 3 | Target g | Target j | Target k | Region 3 sum |
| Product Total | PL A sum | PL B sum | PL C sum | Grand Total |

**Mechanism**: System department and regional office both get credit for project success. Neither can succeed without the other.

#### 3. Mechanism: Market-Driven Internal Economics

**The inversion**: Back office is NOT "serving" frontline as a favor. Back office is a **vendor**; frontline is the **customer**.

**Implementation**:
- Frontline (profit center) holds budget
- Back office (cost/expense center) operates on "空耗" (minimum survival budget)
- Gap must be earned by **selling services to frontline projects**
- If back office doesn't serve well, frontline won't "buy" — department starves

**Result**: "机关资源化，资源市场化"
- Resources pool in back office (specialization)
- But they must compete in internal market (accountability)
- Experts earn by deploying to frontline (钟点工 model — paid by the hour/project)

**Why functional heads love frontline deployment**:
- Their high cost is covered by project budget
- Project success adds to their department's业绩
- Staying in office = pure cost burden

### Common Anti-Patterns (from Huawei's 10 Internal Wastes)

| Anti-Pattern | Root Cause | Structural Fix |
|-------------|-----------|---------------|
| 部门墙 (thick walls) | Functional silos, no shared fate | Matrix + dual credit |
| 权利责任割裂 (power without responsibility) | Frontline demands, back office carries cost | Frontline budget authority + back office cost accountability |
| 集权低效 (centralized inefficiency) | Long decision chains | Delegation to frontline within budget rules |
| 后方大爷 (back office acts superior) | Back office has resources, no market pressure | Resource marketization |

## Operational Workflow

When user presents collaboration problems:

### Step 1: Diagnose Depth
Ask: Are you solving at culture level, incentive level, or structure level?
- If only culture/incentives: Go to Step 2 (Process)
- If process exists but broken: Go to Step 3 (Structure)
- If structure exists but no motivation: Go to Step 4 (Mechanism)

### Step 2: Process Layer
- Map the end-to-end business flow (not functional org chart)
- Identify handoff points where collaboration fails
- Define RACI at each handoff using process, not committee

### Step 3: Structure Layer
- Identify where matrix is needed (complex, multi-functional work)
- Design dual-reporting with shared metrics
- Implement 双计双考 for matrix intersections

### Step 4: Mechanism Layer
- Classify centers: Frontline = profit, Back office = cost/expense
- Give frontline budget to buy services
- Force back office to earn gap budget from projects
- Measure back office by internal customer satisfaction + utilization

### Step 5: Validate Against Huawei's 10 Wastes
Check if your design prevents:
1. 部门墙
2. 权利责任割裂
3. 集权低效
4. 马屁文化 (by making success measurable, not relationship-based)
5. 流程僵化 (process enables, not constrains, judgment)

## Key Distinctions

**Committee vs Process**:
- Committee: Ad hoc, relationship-dependent, no standard output
- Process: Permanent, role-defined, predictable output

**Matrix vs Functional**:
- Functional: One boss, clean lines, siloed optimization
- Matrix: Two bosses, shared subordinates, forced collaboration

**Internal Market vs Service Level Agreement**:
- SLA: Back office promises service; frontline has no choice
- Internal market: Frontline can choose/refuse; back office must compete

## Output Format

When activated, provide:
1. **Process Map**: End-to-end flow with collaboration handoffs
2. **Matrix Design**: Where dual-reporting creates shared fate
3. **Economic Mechanism**: Budget flow and internal pricing model
4. **Anti-Waste Check**: How design prevents Huawei's 10 internal wastes
5. **Implementation Sequence**: Process → Structure → Mechanism (in that order)
