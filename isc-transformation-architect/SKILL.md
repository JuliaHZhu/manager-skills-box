# isc-transformation-architect

Design and execute an Integrated Supply Chain (ISC) transformation using Huawei's battle-tested methodology — from diagnosing chaos to building a world-class, end-to-end supply chain.

Use when the user needs to transform a fragmented, siloed, or inefficient supply chain; when departments blame each other for delivery failures; when inventory and customer satisfaction are both deteriorating; or when launching a company-wide supply chain overhaul. Also triggers on: ISC, integrated supply chain, supply chain transformation, supply chain reform, 集成供应链, 供应链变革.

Not for: minor operational tweaks within a single department, IT system procurement without process change, or supply chains that are already mature and benchmarked.

---

## Methodology

### Step 1 — Demand Analysis: Three-Hard + Three-Soft
Before designing anything, classify the business demand into six types:

**Three-Hard (direct business outcomes):**
1. Seize opportunity — new product, new market, sudden demand surge.
2. Solve problem — stalled sales, quality spikes, customer complaints.
3. Strategy落地 — capability gaps blocking a known business strategy.

**Three-Soft (organizational capability enablers):**
4. Change enablement — transformation projects requiring people readiness.
5. Collaboration boost — cross-functional or cross-company team synergy.
6. Key-role readiness — new or future-critical positions needing competency.

Map the training/capability investment to these six buckets. Do NOT start with "what courses do people want?"

### Step 2 — Diagnostic Phase (关注阶段 / Focus)
Spend 50% of analysis time here. Use interviews, document reviews, and data collection to identify problems across three dimensions:
- **Process**: sales/order, planning, procurement, logistics, manufacturing, customer service.
- **Organization**: structure layers, role clarity, culture/communication barriers.
- **IT**: ERP fragmentation, data accuracy, system silos.

Rank problems by priority and interdependency. A typical diagnosis finds 50–80 issues.

### Step 3 — Future-State Design (发明阶段 / Invent)
Design future-state processes based on the SCOR model (Plan → Source → Make → Deliver → Return → Enable).
- Define end-to-end process ownership, not functional silos.
- Specify new roles and responsibilities.
- Identify IT integration requirements (ERP, APS, procurement, CRM).
- Set KPI targets: customer satisfaction, inventory turns, order-to-delivery lead time, total cost.

Target improvement: ~4/5 on a 5-point maturity scale (from current ~2/5).

### Step 4 — Pilot then Scale (推行阶段 / Deploy)
- Pilot on a single product line, region, or customer segment.
- Capture quick wins to build momentum.
- Establish a change steering committee chaired by the CEO/COO. Resistance = removal of blockers.
- Roll out in waves: rigid execution first (先僵化), then optimize (再优化), then institutionalize (后固化).

### Step 5 — Governance & Metrics
Institutionalize four core metrics:
1. Customer satisfaction / on-time delivery rate
2. Inventory turnover
3. Order fulfillment cycle time
4. Total supply chain cost reduction

Review monthly via S&OP meetings with sales, production, procurement, and finance.

## Key Principles

- **No template copying**: ISC must be tailored to the company's products, customers, and geography.
- **IT is a prerequisite**: without integrated systems, process design cannot stick.
- **CEO sponsorship is non-negotiable**: transformation without top authority will fail.
- **Partnership, not adversarial**: move from price-war procurement to strategic supplier collaboration.
