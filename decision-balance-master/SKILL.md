Use when the user faces major strategic decisions, needs to balance growth vs. profit, debates whether to pursue radical or incremental change, or discusses how to build a "management system without life" (无生命管理) that reduces dependence on heroic leaders. Also triggers on discussions of dictatorship vs. democracy in decision-making.

**Not for**: routine operational decisions with clear rules, or personal career choices without organizational context.

## Core Methodology

Huawei's decision philosophy is built on six balancing acts. Each represents a tension that must be managed, not a choice that can be resolved once and for all.

### Balance 1: Growth Maximization vs. Profit Maximization (成长最大化)

Huawei prioritizes growth over short-term profit. "活下去就是硬道理" (survival is the absolute truth).

1. **Three reasons for growth-first**:
   - Without growth, insufficient profit to sustain R&D and talent.
   - Without growth, inability to attract top talent (Matthew effect).
   - Without growth, falling behind competitors leads to death.

2. **"Dare to spend" philosophy**: In strategic markets and R&D, Huawei spends aggressively. Example: When R&D saved 60 million RMB,任正非 ordered it spent on equipment upgrades — not returned as profit.

3. **Reasonable profit, not maximum profit**: Profit is kept at a sustainable level; excess is reinvested. Huawei does not pursue profit maximization at the expense of future positioning.

4. **Anti-cyclical investment**: Invest more during downturns to capture market share when competitors retreat.

### Balance 2: Incremental Reform vs. Revolution (改良而非革命)

任正非: "I am not a radicalist; I am a reformist who advocates continuous improvement."

1. **Small improvements accumulate**: Toyota's philosophy — daily small improvements matter more than occasional big overhauls.
2. **Preserve core capabilities**: Radical revolutions often destroy existing strengths while trying to fix weaknesses.
3. **Minimize organizational trauma**: Gradual change sustains relationships, culture, and operational continuity.
4. **When revolution is necessary**: Only when the existing system is fundamentally misaligned with survival.

5. **Market collective resignation model (1996)**: All正职干部 submitted both述职 and辞职 reports, then competed for reappointment. This was a controlled, structured shock — not an unplanned purge.

### Balance 3: Living Management vs. Lifeless Management (无生命管理)

Goal: Build a system that runs without the founder's daily intervention.

1. **From hero to system**: Early Huawei relied on任正非's personal drive. Later, it built IPD, ISC, IFS, LTC — systems that outlast individuals.
2. **Process as DNA**: "These lifeless management systems will become richer and more perfect as generations pass. Thousands of years from now, they will still be vibrant. That is the life of the enterprise."
3. **Standardization**:制度化 → 流程化 → 标准化 → 信息化 → 专业化.
4. **Decouple from people**: Reduce dependence on specific individuals for talent, technology, and capital.

### Balance 4: Lead by Half a Step (领先半步)

"Technology that is too advanced lacks a market; technology that lags lacks competitiveness."

1. **The IS95 vs. CDMA2000 lesson**: Huawei bet on the more advanced CDMA2000, but the market chose IS95 first.华为 lost the bid because it was "three steps ahead."
2. **Half-step rule**: Lead competitors by just enough to win customers, not so much that the market is unprepared.
3. **Engineering merchants, not scientists**: "We have no academicians, only '院土' — engineers who sell." Technology must convert to commercial success.
4. **5% rotation**: Require 5% of R&D staff to rotate to marketing annually, and vice versa, to maintain market sensitivity.

### Balance 5: Dictatorship vs. Democracy (独裁与民主)

1. **Founder's dictatorship in early stage**: Necessary for speed and focus.任正非's decisive orders (e.g., "make them feel the pain" on quality) built culture fast.
2. **Democracy needs dictatorship as soil**: "Democracy grows from dictatorship. Without dictatorship, democracy cannot emerge from anarchy."
3. **Limited democracy + moderate centralization**:华为's current model. Collective决策 prevents "one person lifts the nation, one person sinks it." But excessive democracy leads to inefficiency and collective inaction.
4. **Personal意见 channels**:华为 allows干部 to send personal views to a dedicated邮箱, but prohibits联合签名 or unauthorized public postings.
5. **Perfect balance**: "Democratic decision-making, dictatorial execution."

### Balance 6: Lead the Direction, Energize the Organization (方向大致正确，组织充满活力)

1. **Direction is roughly correct**: No one can predict the future perfectly.王安, Kodak, Nokia failed because their directions were wrong.微软 recovered by catching cloud computing.
2. **Organizational vitality compensates for directional error**: A vibrant organization learns fast, adjusts fast, and executes fast.
3. **Decision-making vitality is top-to-bottom**: Not just frontline execution, but the decision-making team's own energy, openness, and willingness to self-critique.
4. **The dragon dance metaphor**: The dragon head (direction) must rise, but the dragon body (organization) must twist with power for the whole dragon to come alive.

## STOP Checkpoints

- **STOP if** a decision pursues profit maximization without considering growth trajectory and competitive position.
- **STOP if** a reform plan attempts to change everything at once without protecting core capabilities.
- **STOP if** the organization remains dependent on a single leader's daily decisions after reaching scale.
- **STOP if** R&D pursues technical elegance without market validation.
- **STOP if** decision-making is either pure dictatorship (no input) or pure democracy (no execution).

## Anti-Patterns (Blacklist)

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails | Correct Practice |
|--------------|--------------|------------------|
| Profit-first at all costs | Starves future growth; loses talent | Growth-first, reasonable profit |
| Revolutionary overhaul | Destroys capability; traumatizes org | Incremental reform, continuous improvement |
| Founder-dependent management | Risk of collapse upon succession | Build lifeless management systems |
| Technology-for-technology's-sake | No market; wasted R&D | Engineering merchant mindset; half-step lead |
| Pure democracy in execution | Endless debate; no action | Democratic decision, dictatorial execution |
