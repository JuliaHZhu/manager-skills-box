# Cadre Rotation Designer

Design a cadre rotation and "zigzag growth" (之字形成长) system that breaks silos, prevents organizational sclerosis, and builds versatile leaders through multi-domain experience — while protecting deep expertise in non-leadership tracks.

## When to Use

When the user needs to:
- Design a rotation system for high-potential managers and experts
- Prevent "straight-line growth" (直线成长) that produces narrow specialists who can't see end-to-end
- Break down departmental walls and reduce本位主义 (localism)
- Decide who must rotate, who should stay put, and how to manage transitions
- Build an internal talent market that lets people move without destroying stability

## Core Concepts

**Zigzag Growth (之字形成长)**: A career path that moves through multiple functions and geographies in a zigzag pattern, not straight up one ladder. Produces leaders with端到端视角 and cross-domain judgment.

**Four Employee Categories**:
1. **Management (管理类)**: Must rotate; leadership requires breadth.
2. **Expert (专家类)**: Must rotate to some degree; senior experts need cross-functional exposure.
3. **Staff (职员类)**: Fixed positions; "干一行、爱一行、专一行". No rotation needed.
4. **Operations (作业类)**: Fixed positions; stability and repetition create value.

**Rotation Triggers**:
- Time-based: Manager tenure < 3 years in one role before eligible for promotion to next level
- Event-based: New business launch, post-merger integration, crisis response
- Development-based: Identified high-potential talent in继任计划

**Internal Talent Market**: A platform where employees can view and apply for internal openings without needing their current manager's permission in certain cases.

## Operating Procedure

### Step 1: Define Rotation Scope

Decide **who rotates** based on the four categories:

| Category | Rotation Required? | Rationale |
|----------|-------------------|-----------|
| Management | Yes — mandatory | Leaders need multi-domain judgment |
| Expert | Yes — limited | Senior experts need cross-functional context |
| Staff | No | Deep specialization and efficiency matter more |
| Operations | No | Stability and mastery reduce defects |

Set the **rotation intensity**:
- ** light**: Cross-functional project participation without changing job
- ** moderate**: 1–2 year lateral moves within same geography
- ** intensive**: Multi-year, multi-country, multi-function zigzag for航母舰长 candidates

### Step 2: Design Rotation Paths

Build standard zigzag paths for key leadership pipelines:

**Example — Regional GM Path**:
1. R&D engineer (2 years)
2. Product manager (2 years)
3. Overseas technical support (2 years)
4. Small-country sales lead (2 years)
5. Medium-country country manager (3 years)
6. Large-country / region GM

**Example — Functional VP Path**:
1. Finance analyst
2. Business controller in a region
3. M&A project lead
4. CFO of a business unit
5. Group CFO

**Rules**:
- No rotation for rotation's sake; every move must serve a business need or a documented development gap
- Minimum stay: 1–2 years to produce real results; maximum stay: 3 years before mandatory move or promotion
- Avoid rotating within the same system only; cross-system moves are the point

### Step 3: Create Support Mechanisms

For every rotation, assign:
- **Receiving manager**: Responsible for onboarding, 90-day integration plan, and first evaluation
- **Mentor**: A senior leader who has done a similar rotation, meets monthly for the first 6 months
- **Knowledge bridge**: 30-day handover period where outgoing and incoming managers overlap

For the employee:
- Pre-rotation briefing on new role's KPIs, stakeholders, and cultural norms
- Language or technical prep if moving to a new domain or country
- 90-day check-in with HRBP to surface integration issues early

### Step 4: Build the Internal Talent Market

Design a platform/process with these features:
- **Open postings**: All internal roles posted before external search begins
- **Self-application**: Employees can apply without current manager blocking (though courtesy notice is expected)
- **Manager non-retaliation policy**: Current managers who block development moves without cause face consequences
- **Talent release obligation**: Business units must release identified high-potentials for rotation when HR calls
- **Repatriation guarantee**: Rotated employees have a right to return to equivalent or better roles if the new role fails

### Step 5: Manage "Up and Down" with Fairness

Rotation must include genuine mobility:
- **Up**: Promotion after successful rotation
- **Down**: Willing降级 to lower-stress roles for health or life-stage reasons, with易岗易薪 (pay adjusts to new role, but equity/benefits protected)
- **Out**: Those who fail in new roles return to talent market or face exit

Protect rotated employees from stigma:
- Frame downward moves as "strategic role shaping" not "demotion"
- Maintain equity vesting and long-term benefits regardless of role level
- Publicize cases where senior leaders took lateral or downward moves and later re-ascended

### Step 6: Monitor and Prevent Dysfunctions

Track rotation health metrics:
- Rotation completion rate (% who finish planned tenure)
- Post-rotation promotion rate within 2 years
- Rotation satisfaction (both employee and receiving manager)
- Business impact of rotated managers vs. non-rotated peers

Watch for these dysfunctions:
- **Tourism**: Rotating too fast to learn anything; fix with minimum stay rules
- **Parking lot**: Rotating poor performers to get rid of them; fix with clear performance requirements before rotation eligibility
- **Revolve door**: Same people rotating among the same clique of jobs; fix by requiring cross-system moves
- **Abandonment**: Receiving managers won't invest in rotators because they leave soon; fix by making rotation development a managerial KPI

## Anti-Patterns

- **Universal rotation**: Forcing everyone to rotate destroys deep expertise. Staff and operations roles should be stable.
- **Rotation as punishment**: Using a tough overseas posting to边缘化 someone destroys trust in the system.
- **No repatriation path**: People won't volunteer for hardship posts if they fear being stranded.
- **Ignoring family impact**: International rotation without family support creates burnout and attrition.
- **Static job descriptions**: Rotating someone into a role that hasn't changed in 5 years teaches nothing new.
