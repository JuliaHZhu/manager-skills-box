# supply-chain-resilience-guardian

Build supply chain resilience and business continuity capability to survive extreme shocks — trade wars, geopolitical bans, natural disasters, and supplier collapses. Based on Huawei's "Noah's Ark" chip strategy and 2012 Lab philosophy.

Use when the user faces geopolitical supply risk, single-source dependencies, needs a backup plan for critical components, or wants to design a "spare tire" system for supply continuity. Also triggers on: supply chain risk, business continuity, BCM, 备胎, 芯片战略, supply resilience, trade war survival, supplier backup.

Not for: routine supplier quality issues solvable by normal QA, or pure financial hedging without operational backup.

---

## Methodology

### Step 1 — Strategic Foresight & Scenario Planning
Establish a dedicated strategic risk team (e.g., Huawei's 2012 Lab model) whose sole job is to think about existential threats:
- Map geopolitical, technological, and natural-disaster scenarios.
- Set a time horizon: what could kill us in 3 years? 5 years? 10 years?
- Maintain a "red team" that constantly challenges the assumption that current suppliers will remain available.

### Step 2 — Critical Component Audit
Classify every component by risk exposure:
- **Strategic / monopoly**: only 1–2 global sources (e.g., advanced chips, OS).
- **Important / concentrated**: limited sources but substitutable with effort.
- **General / competitive**: many sources, easy to switch.

For strategic components, mandate a "self-sufficiency ratio" target even if uneconomical today.

### Step 3 — Dual-Source + Backup Development
For every strategic component, enforce:
- Primary supplier (performance-optimized).
- Secondary supplier (qualified, lower share, kept warm).
- In-house backup program ("spare tire"): funded continuously, judged by readiness — not revenue.

Huawei's rule: "Even if the chip has no commercial use today, keep developing it. It is a strategic flag."

### Step 4 — Strategic Stockpiling
When threat signals appear (e.g., sanctions, export-control chatter):
- Pre-position 6–12 months of inventory for critical items.
- Lock in supplier capacity with long-term orders or prepayments.
- Accelerate qualification of alternative suppliers (domestic or non-traditional).

### Step 5 — Business Continuity Management (BCM)
Build institutional BCM, not just inventory:
- Supplier geographic diversification (avoid concentration in one country/region).
- Multi-supply-center network so no single node failure halts global delivery.
- Emergency response protocols with CEO-level activation triggers.
- Regular stress tests: simulate a key supplier shutdown for 90 days.

### Step 6 — Ecosystem Cultivation
When backup suppliers are immature:
- Invest capital, equipment, and experts into domestic/alternative suppliers.
- Accept higher cost and lower initial quality to build long-term capability.
- Share demand forecasts and co-develop technology so the backup becomes competitive.

---

## Key Principles

- **Resilience before efficiency**: a fragile but efficient supply chain is a liability.
- **Spare tires are judged by existence, not mileage**: backup programs must survive budget cuts.
- **Signals precede shocks**: the window to act is before the ban, not after.
- **Ecosystem health = your health**: if your suppliers die, you die.
