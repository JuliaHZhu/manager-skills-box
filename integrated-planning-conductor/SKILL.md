# integrated-planning-conductor

Run an integrated planning and S&OP (Sales & Operations Planning) system where planning is the "commander" that orchestrates sales, R&D, procurement, and production — not a passive data-transmission desk.

Use when demand and supply are chronically misaligned; when sales blames production for misses while warehouses overflow; when forecasts are always wrong and nobody owns the number; or when building a formal S&OP rhythm. Also triggers on: S&OP, 销售与运作计划, integrated planning, demand planning, supply planning, 计划是龙头, forecast accuracy,产销协同.

Not for: environments with perfectly stable demand and infinite supply flexibility, or when the problem is purely a supplier quality issue unrelated to planning.

---

## Methodology

### Step 1 — Elevate Planning to a Strategic Function
Treat planning as a first-class function, not a back-office support role:
- Give planning organizational authority equal to sales and production.
- Planner mission: integrate strategic intent, sales forecasts, customer orders, capacity, and supplier capability into ONE executable plan.
- Eliminate the "information二传手" mentality — planners must add analytical value, not just forward emails.

### Step 2 — Build the 18-Month Rolling Plan
Institutionalize a rolling planning horizon:
- **Months 1–6**: frozen execution plan — directs procurement and production directly. Must be highly accurate.
- **Months 7–12**: tactical adjustment window — capacity and supplier contracts are negotiable.
- **Months 13–18**: strategic outlook — guides capital expenditure and supplier development.

Roll forward every month. Never let the plan go stale.

### Step 3 — Run the S&OP Meeting Cycle
Hold a monthly S&OP meeting chaired by a senior executive (VP/COO level) with mandatory attendance from:
- Sales / Marketing
- R&D / Product Management
- Procurement / Supply
- Production / Manufacturing
- Finance

Agenda (strict timebox):
1. **Demand review**: validate forecast accuracy, adjust for promotions/new products.
2. **Supply review**: confirm capacity, material availability, and logistics constraints.
3. **Reconciliation**: identify gaps between demand and supply; decide on actions (capacity add, customer priority, inventory build, or demand shaping).
4. **Financial impact**: validate revenue, margin, and cash-flow implications of the chosen plan.
5. **Executive decision**: lock the plan; disputes are resolved by the chair, not by escalation wars.

### Step 4 — Strategy-Driven Differentiation
Before operational planning, set clear policies:
- **Customer segmentation**: strategic / core / growth / tail — each gets a different service level and risk posture.
- **Product segmentation**: cash cow / star / question mark / dog — align supply strategy (MTS vs MTO vs PTO).
- **Inventory strategy**: identify which products use capacity-as-buffer vs inventory-as-buffer.
- **Delivery strategy**: match PTO / MTO / MTS / VMI to customer-product combinations.

Without these policies, planning becomes a pure firefighting exercise.

### Step 5 — Information Accuracy & Early Warning
Build upstream demand sensing:
- Share sell-through data with planners, not just sell-in.
- Capture customer project pipelines (not just POs) for B2B.
- Monitor component market intelligence (lead times, allocation, pricing) as a radar system.
- Flag abnormal orders and demand spikes for human review before they pollute the plan.

### Step 6 — Performance Closed Loop
Track planning effectiveness monthly:
- Forecast accuracy (MAPE) by product family.
- Plan attainment rate (% of plan actually produced and shipped).
- Inventory turns and days of supply.
- Emergency order / expedite cost.

Feed deviations back into the next cycle as improvement inputs.

---

## Key Principles

- **Planning is the commander**: if planning is weak, the whole supply chain fires in random directions.
- **A bad plan consistently executed beats a perfect plan never agreed upon**: S&OP's value is alignment, not optimization.
- **Differentiation before standardization**: one plan for all customers/products guarantees mediocrity for all.
- **Data quality is a prerequisite**: garbage-in, garbage-out applies multiplied across a long supply chain.
