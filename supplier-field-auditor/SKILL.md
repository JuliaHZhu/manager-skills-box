# supplier-field-auditor

Conduct systematic on-site supplier audits to qualify new suppliers or drive improvement at existing ones.

## When to Use

- The user needs to audit a supplier's factory or facility on-site
- The user is qualifying a new supplier and wants a structured evaluation framework
- The user needs supplier audit forms or checklists
- The user is preparing a cross-functional team for a supplier site visit
- The user wants to evaluate supplier capabilities across equipment, quality, process control, and management

## Pre-Audit Preparation

### Step 1 — Clarify Audit Purpose

Determine whether the audit is for:
- **New supplier qualification**: gate for entry into approved vendor list
- **Existing supplier improvement**: diagnose specific problem areas (quality, delivery, capability gaps)

The purpose determines audit depth, sampling scope, and follow-up actions.

### Step 2 — Define Product Scope

- Identify exact products/materials/equipment under audit
- Different products have different critical control points and evaluation criteria

### Step 3 — Understand Manufacturing Process

- Review the supplier's process flow before the visit
- Identify key quality control points (critical-to-quality characteristics)
- Prepare process-specific questions

### Step 4 — Review Internal Standards

Gather all relevant internal standards:
- Procurement specifications
- Quality requirements (IQC standards, AQL levels)
- System/合规 requirements (ISO, environmental, safety)
- Any customer-specific requirements that cascade to suppliers

### Step 5 — Prepare Audit Toolkit

| Form | Purpose | Key Contents |
|------|---------|-------------|
| **Supplier Basic Information Survey** | Pre-visit data collection | Company name, address, legal contact, functional contacts (quality, sales, tech, logistics), production capacity, key equipment, main customers |
| **Supplier Audit Form** | On-site scoring/observation | Checklist covering manpower, machine, material, method, information management; document review; process observation |
| **Supplier Evaluation Form** | Post-visit consensus decision | Multi-department scoring and recommendation (approve / conditional approve / reject) |

## On-Site Audit: 10 Evaluation Factors

Evaluate each factor through direct observation, document review, and interviews.

### 1. Equipment
- Age, condition, cleanliness, layout
- Quantity of materials in production vs. rated capacity
- Current utilization level

### 2. Equipment Management Capability
- Compare with other suppliers producing the same product
- Evidence of preventive maintenance, calibration records
- Impact of sudden demand changes on orderliness

### 3. Technical Capability
- R&D potential and engineering capability
- Ability to support customer service levels
- Technical documentation completeness

### 4. Business Process / Logistics
- Efficiency of process flow and material handling
- Evidence of bottlenecks or waste
- Improvement responsiveness

### 5. Employee Morale
- Workforce attitude and engagement
- Management visibility and fairness perception
- Impact on quality, delivery, and flexibility

### 6. Process Control
- Planning and control systems in use
- Ability to locate and track orders in real time
- Order priority rules
- Inventory and order accuracy controls
- Capacity utilization vs. committed lead times

### 7. Quality Control
- Existence and staffing of quality function
- Quality reporting mechanisms
- Appropriateness of process control systems
- Use of statistical process control (SPC) tools
- Traceability systems

### 8. Purchasing (Supplier's Own Supply Chain)
- How the supplier organizes its procurement
- Adequacy of procurement staffing
- Relationship with its own suppliers
- Supplier's credit ranking with its vendors

### 9. Supplier Commitment
- Interest level in the buyer's business
- Topics that engage senior management
- Investment in quality and customer service

### 10. Technology (IT and Digital)
- Modernity of processing technology
- Use of information systems (ERP, MES, traceability)
- E-commerce / EDI capability
- Technology roadmap for product/service improvement

## Post-Audit Process

### Step 6 — Photo Documentation
- Take photos of key equipment, processes, and any abnormalities
- Organize for internal reporting and archiving

### Step 7 — Complete Evaluation Form
- All audit participants (procurement, quality, R&D, supply chain) independently score
- Consolidate into a single recommendation
- Determine next-step management approach:
  - **Approved**: add to AVL (Approved Vendor List)
  - **Conditional**: approve with improvement plan and re-audit timeline
  - **Rejected**: document reasons and communicate decision

### Step 8 — Archive
- File all audit records (survey, audit form, evaluation, photos, corrective action plans)
- Update supplier master data with audit results

## Cross-Functional Participation

Supplier audits should involve:
- **Procurement**: commercial terms, capacity, commitment
- **Quality**: QC systems, defect history, corrective actions
- **R&D / Engineering**: technical capability, process compatibility
- **Supply Chain**: logistics, lead times, inventory practices

**Learning rule**: procurement staff should listen carefully to questions from other functions and understand *why* they ask — this is the fastest way to build multi-dimensional supplier judgment.

## Boundaries & Anti-Patterns

- **Not for**: ongoing supplier performance monitoring (use scorecards instead); legal/contractual disputes; pure price negotiations
- An audit is a snapshot; a supplier that looks good on audit day may degrade later — combine with ongoing performance data
- Do not send a single-function auditor for strategic suppliers; always use cross-functional teams
- Avoid "checklist tourism" where auditors tick boxes without understanding operational reality
- Photo/documentation policies vary by supplier; always request permission and respect confidentiality agreements
- The Basic Information Survey should be sent and returned *before* the on-site visit to maximize audit efficiency
