## Core Methodology

Huawei's frontline empowerment is built on three pillars: appropriate authorization, matrix organization design, and post-authorization supervision.

### Pillar 1: Four Principles of Authorization (授权四大原则)

1. **Appropriate authorization (适当授权)**
   - Right size: too small fails to motivate; too large risks loss of control.
   - Match employee capacity: workload must not exceed ability, energy, or stamina.
   - Task-dependent: heavy, specialized tasks merit more授权; the manager must retain ability to intervene.

2. **Controllable authorization (授权可控)**
   - The manager retains主动权 and flexibility to adjust scope and timing.
   - Huawei principle: "授权而不彻底放权" — authorize but do not abdicate; supervise and intervene when necessary.

3. **Authorization with accountability (带责授权)**
   - Power and responsibility are granted together.
   - The employee has clear authority boundaries but the manager retains ultimate responsibility for organizational outcomes.
   - Individual authority does not extend to全局性问题 (enterprise-wide issues).

4. **Dynamic authorization (动态考量)**
   - **单项授权**: For a specific project; power reverts when the project ends.
   - **条件授权**: Valid only under specific conditions; when conditions change, authority is reassessed.
   - Example: A staff member is given full authority to handle a specific customer complaint until resolution is achieved and the customer is satisfied.

### Pillar 2: Matrix Organization (矩阵型组织)

Why matrix? Three reasons:
1. **Resource sharing**: Specialists serve multiple projects/products without duplication.
2. **Dual focus**: Product/function excellence + customer/market responsiveness.
3. **Flexibility**: Scale up or down without reorganizing departments.

Three forms of matrix:
1. **Weak matrix**: Functional manager has primary authority; project manager coordinates.
2. **Balanced matrix**: Shared authority between functional and project managers.
3. **Strong matrix**: Project manager has primary authority; functional manager provides resources.

Huawei's evolution: from functional silos → weak matrix → strong matrix for customer-facing units ("let those who hear gunfire call the shots").

Transformation path:
1. **Process变革**: Define end-to-end processes first.
2. **组织建设**: Assign personnel to process roles, not just departments.
3. **能力建设**: Train frontline staff to make decisions traditionally reserved for headquarters.
4. **平台建设**: Build shared service platforms that enable frontline autonomy without fragmentation.

### Pillar 3: Four Stages of Authorization Maturity (授权程度)

| Stage | Target | Scope | Manager Role |
|-------|--------|-------|--------------|
| Constrained (制约授权) | New employees | Basic transactional tasks | Direct supervisor |
| Elastic (弹性授权) | Developing staff | Occasional challenging tasks | Coach |
| Partial (不充分授权) | Middle骨干 | Important tasks, project participation | Mentor |
| Full (充分授权) | Core employees / key talent | Strategic tasks with freedom within boundaries | Monitor only |

### Pillar 4: Navy SEALs Model for Frontline Teams (海军陆战队式一线授权)

Principles drawn from special operations teams:
1. **Small, autonomous units**: Frontline teams have decision authority at the point of contact.
2. **Clear mission intent**: Headquarters provides "what" and "why"; frontline decides "how."
3. **Embedded support**: Intelligence, logistics, and fire support are pushed forward, not held back.
4. **Real-time communication**: Horizontal communication among frontline units is as important as vertical reporting.
5. **Accountability after action**: Debrief and learn; do not second-guess decisions made with good intent and available information.

### Pillar 5: Post-Authorization Supervision (授权后监督)

1. **Retain牵制权**: The manager keeps necessary checks to correct course if power divergence occurs.
2. **Reporting mechanisms**: Regular progress reports keep the manager informed without micromanaging.
3. **Process-based control**: Use流程节点 (process milestones) as control points, not personal approval gates.
4. **Exception escalation**: Define what must be escalated; everything else stays at the frontline.

## STOP Checkpoints

- **STOP if** authorization is granted without clear boundaries or accountability.
- **STOP if** the matrix design leaves frontline staff reporting to two bosses with conflicting priorities and no resolution mechanism.
- **STOP if** "empowerment" is only rhetoric — frontline staff still need 5 signatures for routine decisions.
- **STOP if** authorization is given but information, tools, and budget are withheld.

## Anti-Patterns (Blacklist)

| Anti-Pattern | Why It Fails | Correct Practice |
|--------------|--------------|------------------|
| Abdication disguised as empowerment | Manager disappears; employee has no support | Supervision remains, support is provided |
| One-size-fits-all authorization | New hires and veterans get same authority | Four-stage maturity model |
| Matrix without conflict resolution | Employee caught between two bosses | Clear primary reporting line per task |
| Centralized information, decentralized authority | Frontline decides but lacks data | Push intelligence forward |
| Punish frontline mistakes, reward headquarters caution | Frontline stops taking initiative | Tolerate good-faith errors, debrief and learn |
