## Core Methodology

Ren Zhengfei's twelve management tenets, distilled from decades of Huawei practice. Each tenet is a decision heuristic, not a slogan.

### The Twelve Tenets

1. **Survive (活着)**
   - The first duty of a firm is to stay alive, not to maximize profit.
   - Ask: "What threatens our survival?" before asking "How do we grow?"

2. **Wash the saline-alkali soil (洗盐碱地)**
   - Some markets look barren (low profit, high friction) but are where competitors retreat.
   - Persistently cultivate "saline-alkali land"; thin margins force capability improvement.
   - The鸡肋 you endure becomes the moat others cannot cross.

3. **Red Army vs. Blue Army (红军蓝军)**
   - Blue Army plays adversary; Red Army defends.
   - Blue Army must exist at every level, not just as a top-level think tank.
   - Tolerate organized opposition. Self-criticism > criticism of others.

4. **Deep dredge the beach, low build the weir (深淘滩，低作堰)**
   - From Li Bing's 2,000-year-old Dujiangyan irrigation principle.
   - **Deep dredge**: continuously dig out internal waste, corruption, inefficiency.
   - **Low weir**: restrain greed; keep profit margins modest. Share gains with customers and suppliers. A healthy upstream-downstream chain is the firm's lifeblood.

5. **Force through one hole, profit through one hole (力出一孔，利出一孔)**
   - All 150,000 people's energy focused through a single aperture — like rocket exhaust through a Laval nozzle.
   - If energy disperses across too many initiatives, no single breakthrough occurs.
   - Applies to both strategic focus and incentive alignment.

6. **Skill outside poetry (功夫在诗外)**
   - Management excellence comes from broad knowledge (history, philosophy, military science), not just management books.
   - Ren reads no pure management books; he reads history and philosophy.
   - Encourage leaders to read outside their domain.

7. **Bian Que's eldest brother (扁鹊大哥)**
   - Bian Que (famous doctor) said his eldest brother was the best because he prevented illness before it appeared.
   - The best managers prevent crises; they are invisible because nothing goes wrong.
   - Do not celebrate firefighting; celebrate the absence of fires.

8. **No one steps into the same river twice (人不能两次踏入同一条河流)**
   - Heraclitus: everything flows. Past success formulas become future failure traps.
   - Continuous minor reform > sporadic major revolution.
   - Keep changing before the environment forces you to.

9. **Fresh flowers grow from cow dung (鲜花插在牛粪上)**
   - Innovation must build on existing competence, not leap into the void.
   - Use the existing "cow dung" (mature business) as nutrients for new "flowers" (innovation).
   - When the flower grows, it becomes new dung for the next cycle.

10. **Too much complaining harms the gut; take the long view (牢骚太盛防肠断，风物长宜放眼量)**
    - From Mao Zedong's poem to Liu Yazi.
    - Leaders must endure grievances and maintain equanimity.
    - Short-term injustice is the price of long-term perspective.

11. **Still waters run deep (静水潜流)**
    - Surface calm conceals immense force.
    - Avoid flashy announcements; let results speak.
    - The deeper the water, the less splash when a stone is thrown.

12. **Grayscale (灰度)**
    - The world is not black and white; most decisions live in the 18% gray.
    - Openness, compromise, and tolerance are Huawei cultural essences.
    - Apply grayscale to: ownership structure, globalization, customer relations, quality vs. cost, innovation vs. conservatism, employee status.
    - The leader's level is measured by the appropriate grayscale they can hold.

### Application Protocol

When facing a major decision, run the twelve tenets as a quick sanity check:

1. **Survival check** — Does this endanger our ability to stay alive?
2. **Soil check** — Are we avoiding hard markets just because they look unprofitable?
3. **Opposition check** — Have we stress-tested this with an internal adversary?
4. **Dredge/weir check** — Are we cutting internal fat and capping our greed?
5. **Focus check** — Is this aligned with our "one hole"?
6. **Breadth check** — Do we have outside-domain perspectives?
7. **Prevention check** — Are we solving root causes or symptoms?
8. **Change check** — Is our plan based on today's reality or yesterday's?
9. **Foundation check** — Does this build on existing strengths?
10. **Temperament check** — Can we endure the grievances this will cause?
11. **Noise check** — Are we seeking attention or results?
12. **Gray check** — Have we rejected false binaries?

## STOP Checkpoint

Before finalizing a strategic stance, verify at least 6 of the 12 tenets have been explicitly considered.
