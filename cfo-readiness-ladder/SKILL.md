## Purpose
Define the capabilities, experiences, and mindset required for finance professionals to grow from transactional accountants into strategic CFOs who can eventually assume CEO responsibility. Based on Huawei's explicit standard — "A competent CFO should be ready to take over as CEO at any time" — this skill provides a developmental ladder, assessment criteria, and rotation paths.

## When to Use
- When designing a finance talent pipeline and succession plan
- When assessing whether a current CFO is truly strategic or merely operational
- When planning job rotations between finance and business roles
- When coaching high-potential finance managers on their career trajectory
- When setting hiring standards for senior finance positions
- When a CEO transition requires evaluating internal CFO candidates

## When NOT to Use
- For entry-level accounting training (use basic accounting courses)
- For non-finance roles seeking general leadership development
- When the organization is too small (<100 people) to warrant a formal CFO track
- For technical treasury or tax specialization paths (narrower scope)

## Core Standard: The Competent CFO

Huawei's definition of a qualified CFO:
1. **Can take over as CEO at any moment**.
2. **Understands the business deeply** — otherwise only provides low-value accounting services.
3. **Has project management experience** — no project experience, no path to CFO.
4. **Possesses self-driven hunger for growth**.
5. **Reads contracts fluently** — understands commercial terms, acceptance criteria, product configurations.

## The CFO Capability Model

### Dimension 1: Financial Expertise (Foundation)
- **Accounting mastery**: Can close books, produce compliant reports, and explain variances.
- **Treasury literacy**: Understands cash flow, funding, FX, and liquidity management.
- **Tax awareness**: Knows enough to flag risks and opportunities; defers to specialists for execution.
- **Control design**: Can build internal controls that enable rather than obstruct business.

### Dimension 2: Business Acumen (Differentiator)
- **Customer knowledge**: Can name the top 10 customers, their needs, and competitive dynamics.
- **Competitor intelligence**: Understands rival financials and strategic moves.
- **Industry foresight**: Anticipates technology and market shifts that reshape financial requirements.
- **Value-chain economics**: Knows where profit is created and destroyed across the value chain.

### Dimension 3: Strategic Integration (Peak)
- **Planning leadership**: Leads integrated business planning (IBP) connecting strategy to budget to forecast.
- **Investment judgment**: Evaluates capex, M&A, and R&D bets using dynamic competitive analysis, not static NPV.
- **Capital structure design**: Balances debt and equity to support strategic flexibility.
- **Board communication**: Speaks the language of investors, directors, and external stakeholders.

### Dimension 4: Leadership & Influence (Enabler)
- **Cross-functional command**: Earns respect from sales, R&D, and operations — not just finance.
- **Communication clarity**: Translates complex financial concepts into actionable business language.
- **Change leadership**: Drives major transformations (e.g., IFS, shared services) through resistance.
- **Talent development**: Builds a pipeline of finance leaders beneath them.

## The Financial Golden Triangle (财务金三角)
A CFO must balance three dimensions simultaneously:

| Dimension | Meaning | Danger of Neglect |
|-----------|---------|-------------------|
| **Growth (增长性)** | Revenue and market expansion | Pure growth without profit = reckless expansion |
| **Profitability (盈利性)** | Margin and return on capital | Pure profit without growth = harvesting the future |
| **Liquidity (流动性)** | Cash flow and financial flexibility | Pure profit on paper without cash = bankruptcy risk |

**Rule**: KPIs and incentives must reflect all three legs. A bonus tied only to revenue invites reckless discounting. A bonus tied only to profit invites underinvestment. A bonus tied only to cash invites missed growth opportunities.

## The Development Ladder

### Level 1 — Transactional Accountant (0–3 years)
**Scope**: Bookkeeping, reconciliations, expense processing, invoice verification.
**Development focus**: Accuracy, speed, system fluency.
**Rotation target**: Move to a business-facing role (e.g., project accountant, sales finance) within 3 years.

### Level 2 — Business Finance Partner (3–7 years)
**Scope**: Supports a business unit with budgeting, forecasting, and decision support.
**Development focus**: Learn the business deeply, build relationships with non-finance managers.
**Key experience**: Manage at least one full project lifecycle from bid to cash collection.
**Rotation target**: Rotate into a line role (e.g., sales operations, supply chain planning) for 1–2 years.

### Level 3 — Functional Finance Leader (7–12 years)
**Scope**: Heads a finance sub-function (e.g., regional CFO, product line CFO, shared-service director).
**Development focus**: P&L accountability, team leadership, cross-border complexity.
**Key experience**: Lead a finance transformation project (e.g., ERP rollout, shared-service build).
**Rotation target**: Take a P&L-bearing business role (e.g., country manager, BG director) if CEO track is the goal.

### Level 4 — Group CFO Candidate (12+ years)
**Scope**: Group-level finance strategy, capital markets, investor relations, board interaction.
**Development focus**: Enterprise-wide perspective, external stakeholder management, CEO-level presence.
**Key experience**: Successfully navigated a major crisis or turnaround.
**Final test**: Can confidently step into the CEO role on day one if required.

## Rotation Philosophy: "Concrete Structure" (混凝土结构)

Huawei practices deliberate rotation between finance and business roles:
- **Finance → Business**: Teaches finance leaders how decisions are really made at the front line.
- **Business → Finance**: Brings operational reality into financial planning and control.
- **Result**: A leadership cadre that speaks both languages fluently and cannot be fooled by either side.

**Rule**: CFO-track professionals must spend time in roles where they are measured by business outcomes (revenue, delivery, customer satisfaction), not just financial metrics.

## CFO as Monitor-and-Enabler

### Monitoring Responsibility
The CFO organization must act as a "dam" (大坝) — a vertical, independent structure that:
- Filters compliant transactions through.
- Catches non-compliant ones for discussion and correction.
- Reports anomalies broadly (not just to the transaction owner) to create accountability pressure.

### Service Responsibility
Monitoring does NOT mean obstruction. The CFO provides:
- **Fast-path service** for high-integrity business units.
- **Pre-emptive risk warnings** before decisions are irreversible.
- **Decision support** that improves business outcomes, not just records them.

### Communication Discipline
- **Low-key in person**: Discuss issues privately with business partners first.
- **High-key in writing**: Publish risk reports and variances prominently when private discussion fails.

## STOP Checkpoints
Before promoting a finance leader to CFO or CFO-candidate status, verify:
1. [ ] Have they managed at least one complete project from bid to cash?
2. [ ] Can they read and critique a customer contract without legal assistance?
3. [ ] Have they spent time in a business role with P&L or revenue accountability?
4. [ ] Do they balance the Financial Golden Triangle (growth, profit, liquidity) in their recommendations?
5. [ ] Can they articulate the top 3 strategic risks facing the company in business language?
6. [ ] Would they be credible as CEO if required to step in tomorrow?

## Output Format
When invoked, provide:
1. **Current State Assessment**: Where is the finance leader on the 4-level ladder?
2. **Gap Analysis**: Missing experiences, skills, or exposures vs. the CFO standard.
3. **Development Plan**: Specific rotations, projects, and training for the next 2–3 years.
4. **Golden Triangle Dashboard**: How the leader's current recommendations score on growth/profit/liquidity balance.
5. **Monitoring-vs-Service Profile**: Whether the leader leans toward control or enablement, and how to correct imbalance.
6. **Succession Timeline**: Estimated readiness for CFO role and any derailers to watch.
