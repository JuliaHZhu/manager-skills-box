# Responsibility Center Operator

Design and operate a responsibility-center management system that delegates operational authority while strengthening financial control, based on Huawei's practice of rejecting阿米巴 (Amoeba) internal profit-center fragmentation.

## When to Use

Activate when the user discusses:
- 责任中心 / profit center / cost center / expense center / investment center
- 下放经营权 + 加强监控权
- 内部结算 / 转移定价 / 成本价结算
- 阿米巴 vs 责任中心 / 为什么不用阿米巴
- 经营指标分解 / 预算管控 / 代表处利润中心
- 机关资源化、资源市场化
- 一线呼唤炮火 / 听得到炮声的人决策

Do NOT activate for: pure financial accounting, personal budgeting, or阿米巴 implementation (this skill explicitly contrasts with and argues against阿米巴-style internal profit centers).

## Core Concepts

### 1. Four Types of Responsibility Centers

| Type | Definition | Key Metrics | Huawei Example |
|------|-----------|-------------|----------------|
| **Investment Center** | Controls investment, profit, and costs | ROIC, strategic alignment | Corporate/BU level |
| **Profit Center** | Controls revenue and costs | Revenue, profit, market share | 代表处 (regional office), 产品线 (product line) |
| **Cost Center** | Controls costs only | Cost efficiency, quality, delivery | Manufacturing, after-sales service |
| **Expense Center** | Controls expenses only | Budget adherence, service quality | R&D platforms, support functions |

**Huawei's core principle**: Minimize internal profit centers. Only customer-facing units (代表处) and product lines are profit centers. Everything else is cost/expense center to reduce internal friction.

### 2. Why Huawei Rejected Amoeba (阿米巴)

**Amoeba assumption**: Each unit is a profit center; internal transfer pricing creates healthy competition.

**Huawei's reality**:
- Internal transfer pricing leads to endless bargaining, not value creation
- Energy shifts from "serving customers" to "negotiating with colleagues"
- Chinese cultural context lacks Japan's shame-based inhibition against exploiting internal partners
- 徐直军 explicitly opposed internal profit center / virtual profit center design in 2011

**Huawei's alternative**: Responsibility centers with **cost-based internal settlement** (no markup). Two core principles:
1. Everything customer-centered (reduce internal博弈)
2. All internal settlement at cost price (no markup without collective negotiation)

### 3. Delegation + Control Framework

**下放经营权 (Delegate operational authority)**:
- Let those who hear the gunfire call the shots
- Frontline units (代表处) have budget authority to summon resources
- Enables rapid decision-making for 180,000-person organization

**加强监控权 (Strengthen monitoring)**:
- IFS (Integrated Financial Services) transformation BEFORE LTC transformation
- Responsibility center + planning/budgeting/forecasting create constraint mechanisms
- "从心所欲不逾矩" — freedom within clearly defined rules

### 4. Profit Center Design

**Why 代表处 are profit centers**:
- End-to-end customer responsibility
- Prevents regional保守 (conservatism) vs BG激进 (aggressiveness) conflict through designed tension

**Why product lines are profit centers**:
- Product line managers embedded in each 代表处
- Dual reporting: solid line to 代表处, dotted line to product line
- Aligns product and regional interests — "same number, same fate"
- R&D总裁 flies to frontline for major bids

**Manufacturing as cost center, not profit center**:
- Manufacturing cost only 1-2% of revenue
- First priority: quality; second: delivery; third: cost
- Heavy outsourcing reduces manufacturing strategic weight

### 5. Budget and Performance Design

**Process**:
1. Define KPI structure for each organization type first (rules before numbers)
2. Let conservative and aggressive units debate targets (finance facilitates, doesn't dictate)
3. Finalize specific numbers after structural agreement

**Profit center criteria**:
- Performance judged by market results (e.g., 海思 chip — 麒麟920 failed = bad; 麒麟925 in Mate7 = good)
- Platform products judged by market adoption of downstream products

## Operational Workflow

When user asks about responsibility center design, follow this sequence:

### Step 1: Organizational Classification
Map the user's organization into the four responsibility center types:
- Which units face customers directly? → **Profit center candidates**
- Which units provide internal services? → **Cost/Expense centers**
- Which units make strategic investment decisions? → **Investment centers**

### Step 2: Internal Settlement Rule Design
- Settle at **cost price** (no markup by default)
- If markup required, must be collectively negotiated with transparent methodology
- Eliminate internal profit-center mentality for support functions

### Step 3: Authority Delegation Map
- What decisions can frontline make without approval?
- What resources can they summon?
- What are the spending thresholds?

### Step 4: Monitoring Mechanism
- Budget constraint (planning/budgeting/forecasting)
- Financial shared services (6 global centers handling 300+ entities, closing in 2 days)
- Unified COA (Chart of Accounts) for transparent consolidation

### Step 5: Performance Metrics by Center Type
- Profit centers: Revenue, profit, market share, cash flow
- Cost centers: Quality, delivery, cost (in that priority order)
- Expense centers: Service level, budget adherence, internal customer satisfaction

## Key Distinctions

**Huawei Responsibility Center vs Amoeba**:
- **Amoeba**: Many profit centers, internal transfer pricing with markup, culture-based fairness
- **Huawei**: Few profit centers, cost-based settlement, rule-based fairness, customer-facing focus

**Frontline vs Back office**:
- Frontline (profit center): Has budget, can summon炮火, measured by market results
- Back office (cost/expense center): Serves frontline, budget gap must be earned from frontline projects

## Output Format

When activated, provide:
1. **Classification Map**: Which units should be which center type
2. **Settlement Rules**: Cost-based or negotiated markup mechanisms
3. **Delegation Charter**: What frontline can decide autonomously
4. **Monitoring Dashboard**: Key metrics and consolidation approach
5. **Anti-Amoeba Checklist**: Why this design avoids internal bargaining pitfalls
