## Core Methodology

Three goal-management frameworks, each with distinct适用场景, plus Huawei's procedural justice layer.

### Framework 1: MBO (Management by Objectives)

- **Origin**: Peter Drucker, *The Practice of Management* (1954).
- **Core logic**: Means-end chain.上级目标 = 下级目的; 下级手段 = 上级目标.
- **Process**: Top-down decomposition with bottom-up commitment through negotiation.
- **Key distinction**: 目标分解 (top-down assignment) vs. 目标解码 (bottom-up decoding).
  - Huawei insists on **decoding**: subordinates propose how they will contribute to the上级 target, rather than receiving a forced quota.
- **Strength**: alignment, self-control, commitment through participation.
- **Weakness**: tends to degenerate into KPI gaming when linked too tightly to bonuses.

### Framework 2: Balanced Scorecard (BSC)

- **Origin**: Kaplan & Norton, *The Balanced Scorecard* (1992).
- **Four perspectives**:
  1. **Financial**: revenue growth, free cash flow, ROI, EVA.
  2. **Customer**: market share, customer satisfaction, retention, brand awareness.
  3. **Internal process**: quality, delivery周期, unit cost, time-to-market.
  4. **Learning & growth**: labor productivity, top-talent retention, digitalization level.
- **Design rule**: each perspective's targets must link to strategy; lagging indicators (results) paired with leading indicators (drivers).
- **Huawei application**: "5 Ones" metric for order-to-cash cycle (1 day PO processing, 1 week shipment, 1 month logistics, 1 minute software download, 1 month delivery验收).
- **Strength**: comprehensive, strategy-linked, prevents short-termism.
- **Weakness**: too many indicators (~20); weights get diluted; hard to cascade below SBU level; execution mechanics underdeveloped.

### Framework 3: OKR (Objectives and Key Results)

- **Origin**: Andy Grove at Intel (1971); popularized by John Doerr.
- **Structure**:
  - **Objective**: important, specific, action-oriented, inspiring.
  - **Key Results**: specific, time-bound, challenging but achievable, **measurable and verifiable**.
- **Four "weapons"**:
  1. **Focus & commitment** — prioritize what matters; say no to distractions.
  2. **Alignment & connection** — full transparency; everyone's OKRs visible.
  3. **Accountability tracking** — data-driven regular check-ins.
  4. **Stretch & ambition** — set goals that feel impossible; even 70% achievement is success.
- **Critical rule**: OKRs are **decoupled from compensation**.
  - If bonuses depend on OKR attainment, employees will sandbag goals.
  - Crazy goals should be rewarded for progress, not punished for missing 100%.
- **CFR pairing**: Conversation + Feedback + Recognition for continuous performance management.

### Framework Selection Matrix

| Situation | Recommended Framework | Why |
|-----------|----------------------|-----|
| Large enterprise, SBU-level strategy deployment | BSC + MBO | Comprehensive, proven at scale |
| Startup / high-tech / rapid change environment | OKR | Agility, transparency, stretch innovation |
| Functional department needing clear targets | MBO + KPI | Clarity, accountability, stable environment |
| Post-merger integration needing alignment | BSC | Bridges multiple stakeholders |
| Turnaround requiring breakthrough thinking | OKR | Encourages risk-taking without penalty |

### Procedural Justice in Performance Evaluation (程序公正)

Huawei emphasizes that **fair process** matters more than perfect metrics.

Three pillars:
1. **Collective evaluation (集体评价)** — no single manager decides hiring, firing, rating, or promotion. Decisions are made by a panel.
2. **Result transparency (结果公开)** — publish evaluation results. Sunlight is the best disinfectant against favoritism.
3. **Appeal channel (申诉通道)** — ensure a畅通 path to challenge ratings.

Google's additional practices:
- Upward feedback surveys (manager rated by team).
- Reward well-planned risk even if it fails.

### STOP Checkpoint

Before deploying a goal framework, verify:
- [ ] Have we matched the framework to our organization's size, speed, and culture?
- [ ] If using OKR, are we prepared to decouple it from bonus payout?
- [ ] If using BSC, have we limited total indicators to a manageable number (<15)?
- [ ] If using MBO, are subordinates decoding targets or receiving quotas?
- [ ] Is there a collective-evaluation mechanism to prevent single-point bias?
- [ ] Are evaluation results transparent to the relevant population?
