# Talent Exit & Workforce Balancer

Manage workforce renewal and inter-generational pay equity using Huawei's talent lifecycle approach. Covers three-axe rapid improvement, four exit pathways, and conflict resolution between new and veteran employee compensation.

## When to Use

- When designing humane but firm talent exit mechanisms that avoid organizational aging
- When facing pay inversion (new hires paid more than loyal veterans)
- When you need to improve human-capital ROI quickly without mass layoffs
- When a business unit has become bloated or skill-obsolete

## How It Works

### Axe 1: Probation Management (试用期管理)

- **Standard**: Filter out the bottom 20% during probation. No organization exceeds ~80% hiring accuracy (the "Everest peak"), so at least 20% are mis-hires.
- **Action**: At month 2–3, force-rank probationers; terminate or transfer the bottom 20%.
- **Benefit**: Prevents bad capital from poisoning good capital. Proximity to low performers drags down high performers.

### Axe 2: Early-Career Differentiation (1–3年差别化保留)

- **Critical window**: Year 1–3 is when human-capital investment turns from negative to positive.
- **Risk**: Top performers are most likely to leave just as they become profitable.
- **Action**:
  - Force-rank all 1–3 year employees
  - Identify **Top 30%**: Give differentiated pay, faster rotation, and visible development opportunities
  - Monitor **Bottom 30%**: Coach up or manage out
- **Goal**: Ensure good currency drives out bad (良币驱逐劣币). Retain the rising stars; do not fear losing mediocre performers.

### Axe 3: Mid-Career Exit & Renewal (三分点退出机制)

- **Inflection point**: Around year 6 (3–5 for internet, 8–10 for traditional industries), talent curves diverge:
  - Top 30%: Continue accelerating (core栋梁)
  - Middle 30%: Plateau into reliable contributors
  - Bottom 30–40%: Declining year over year
- **Action**: Categorize the post-inflection cohort into three tracks and activate **exit mechanisms** for the declining layer.
- **Exit is not only firing**: Use the four pathways below.

### Four Exit Pathways (人才退出四方案)

1. **Side-business diversion** (副业分流): Move employees to affiliated or spun-off entities (e.g., Huawei → Huaweitong / joint ventures). Preserves employment while shrinking the core.
2. **Role conversion** (角色转换): Move from execution to advisory, board, or governance roles. Reduce scope while retaining institutional memory.
3. **Early retirement** (提前退休): Accept short-term cost for long-term agility. Package includes honor status, lump-sum compensation, and optional affiliate roles.
4. **Internal entrepreneurship** (内部创业): Fund departures to become partners/suppliers. Company takes equity stake; maintains relationship and values alignment. Best case: build an ecosystem (e.g., OPPO dealers from ex-employees, Tencent investing in "solo-flying penguins").

### Resolving New-Veteran Pay Conflicts (新老员工薪酬矛盾)

When market rates for new hires exceed veteran pay, **do not** universalize raises. Use four levers:

1. **Bonus differential**: Veterans receive higher bonuses due to maturity and network effects; total package parity can be maintained even if base pay lags.
2. **Long-term incentives**: Grant veterans (especially Top 30%) TUP or virtual equity after year 1. New hires rarely receive this immediately.
3. **Signing / transfer premium (3Bonus)**: Pay new hires a separate "signing fee" (e.g., 20% of base) for 2 years only. After expiration, they must earn their way up through the standard merit system.
4. **Time**: If the pay system is sound, within 2–3 years contribution-based pricing replaces cost-based pricing. Advanced-degree hires who under-contribute will fall behind high-performing veterans naturally.

> Principle: Compensation shifts from **cost-based pricing** (degree/experience) at entry to **value-based pricing** (contribution) once work begins.
