# Cadre Development System

Design and evolve a systematic cadre development system that progresses from basic management training (MST) to role-based systematic development (SLD) to integrated leadership development (ILD) linked with selection, evaluation, and appointment.

## When to Use

When the user needs to:
- Build a staged cadre development program instead of one-off training courses
- Shift from generic competency-based training to role-based practical development
- Design specific turning programs for first-line managers, middle managers, and senior leaders
- Integrate cadre development with selection, evaluation, and appointment systems
- Avoid the "three motions" problem (moved before training, excited during training, motionless after training)

## Core Concepts

**Three Stages (MST → SLD → ILD)**:
- **MST (Management Skill Training)**: Basic managerial skills — team building, situational leadership, appreciation of individual differences. Entry-level, short courses.
- **SLD (Systematic Leadership Development)**: Role-based, targeted development by job family. From generic competency models to "what does this role actually do?" curricula. Includes action plans, assignments, and答辩验收.
- **ILD (Integrated Leadership Development)**: Cadre development integrated with talent review, selection, evaluation, and appointment. Strategic预备队 model where selection → training → combat → evaluation → appointment form a closed loop.

**Role-Based over Competency-Based**: Instead of teaching "influence" or "communication" abstractly, teach "how to execute strategy as a country GM" or "how to integrate cross-cultural resources as a regional director". Develop based on key business activities the role must perform.

**Three-Track Progression**: Soldier (individual contributor) → Hero (high-performing expert) → Squad Leader (first-line manager) → General (senior leader). Each stage has different skill emphasis.

## Operating Procedure

### Step 1: Diagnose Current Stage

Assess which stage your organization is in:

| Symptom | Likely Stage |
|---------|-------------|
| Training is ad-hoc single courses; no clear linkage to roles | MST |
| Courses exist but graduates don't apply learning; HR can't explain why these courses vs others | MST |
| Role-based curricula exist for key jobs; action plans and验收 are standard | SLD |
| Development is decoupled from selection and appointment; anyone can attend if they have time | SLD |
| Selection, training, evaluation, and appointment are coordinated; strategic reserves feed talent pipelines | ILD |

**Guideline**: Start at MST if resources are limited. Move to SLD once basic managerial literacy is universal. Advance to ILD only when HR, business, and executive systems can collaborate.

### Step 2: Design MST Foundation (If Needed)

For organizations without basic managerial training:
- **Import first**: License proven courses (situational leadership, team building, case-based employee management). Don't build from scratch.
- **Teach philosophy of use**: What do our managers most lack right now? Teach that first.
- **Force action plans**: Every training ends with a written action plan. Track implementation for 30–90 days.

Target: 1–3 days face-to-face + 3–6 months on-the-job practice.

### Step 3: Build SLD Role-Based Programs

For key management roles (e.g., country GM, CFO, R&D director):

1. **Role decomposition**: Break the role into 4–6 key responsibility areas (e.g., "strategy executor", "cross-cultural team builder", "resource integrator").
2. **Activity mapping**: For each responsibility, list 3–5 key business activities.
3. **Curriculum design**: Build courses that teach *how to perform those activities*. All content must map back to a specific role activity.
4. **Differentiated methods**:
   - **Knowledge/skills**: Classroom + online + case study
   - **Core capabilities** (e.g., strategic thinking): Selection + job rotation, not classroom
   - **Key experiences**: Real projects and rotations, not simulations
5. **Action learning + defense**: Each module ends with an action plan; program ends with a defense答辩 where the manager presents what they practiced and learned.

Target: ~16 days face-to-face across 3 modules + 3–6 months inter-module practice.

### Step 4: Advance to ILD Integration

Link cadre development to the five cadre management subsystems:

| Subsystem | What it tells development |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| **Inventory (盘点)** | How many successors do we need? Where are the gaps? |
| **Standards** | What capabilities and experiences should development target? |
| **Selection** | Who should enter development? (Don't water the wrong plants.) |
| **Evaluation** | Did development actually improve performance and readiness? |
| **Appointment** | Where should graduates be placed to maximize impact? |

Design strategic预备队 programs where:
- Selection gate is controlled by干部部/HR, not training department alone
- Training content is exactly what combat requires (训战结合)
- Evaluation happens continuously during training and combat
- Appointment uses evaluation results directly for placement or promotion

### Step 5: Design Level-Specific Turning Programs

| Level | Program | Focus | Duration |
|-------|---------|-------|----------|
| Individual → First-line manager | FLMP (First-Line Manager Program) | "管人" — team leadership, role transition | 3 days + 3–6 months practice |
| High-potential individual → Project manager | 青训班 (Youth Training Camp) | "管事" — end-to-end project management | 3–5 days + project实践 |
| Middle manager → Senior leader | 高研班 (Advanced Seminar) | "悟道" — philosophy, culture, strategic thinking | 1–2 weeks + thesis/defense |

**高研班 special rules**:
- Charge personal tuition (e.g., ¥20,000) to signal self-investment
- Completion is a prerequisite for promotion to senior roles
- Curriculum focuses on company core values, management philosophy, and战略文件 — not skills

### Step 6: Maintain Downward Compatibility

Reaching SLD or ILD does not eliminate MST. Continue offering single courses and generic training as needed. The three stages are **downward compatible** — higher stages include lower-stage tools but add integration.

## Anti-Patterns

- **Competency-model trap**: Hiring a consultancy to build an abstract competency model, then mapping generic courses to it. Results in "听起来有道理，做起来没用".
- **Graduation without application**: Training satisfaction scores are high, but behavior doesn't change. Fix by mandating action plans and答辩.
- **Open enrollment**: Allowing anyone with free time to attend expensive role-based programs. Fix by linking enrollment to selection systems.
- **Classroom-only senior development**: Teaching strategy via PowerPoint to VPs. Fix with高研班-style philosophy研讨 and self-funded commitment.
- **Ignoring values/culture**: Focusing only on skills (专) without reinforcing core values (红). Every program must include role认知 + values研讨.
