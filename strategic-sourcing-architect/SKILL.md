# strategic-sourcing-architect

Design strategic procurement and supplier partnerships that go beyond price negotiation to create competitive advantage, innovation access, and supply security. Based on Huawei's strategic sourcing five features and six-step procurement strategy methodology.

Use when procurement needs to shift from transactional buying to strategic value creation; when key suppliers must become innovation partners; or when designing a multi-year procurement roadmap aligned with company strategy. Also triggers on: strategic sourcing, procurement strategy, 战略采购, supplier partnership, 采购战略, category strategy, procurement roadmap.

Not for: routine spot buying, low-value indirect procurement, or supplier selection where price is the only criterion.

---

## Methodology

### Step 1 — Define Strategic Sourcing Five Features
Ensure the procurement organization understands and pursues all five:
1. **Business-success oriented**: every sourcing decision must help the company win in the market.
2. **Joint innovation**: co-develop technology with strategic suppliers; share IP and risk.
3. **Mutual trust & long-term win-win**: help suppliers survive downturns (e.g., early payments); expect priority support during shortages.
4. **Supply resilience**: build BCM with key suppliers; demand continuity plans and disaster-readiness.
5. **Digital minimal-transaction**: use digital platforms to simplify PO-to-payment chains; free buyers for value-added work.

### Step 2 — Apply the Procurement Strategy Six-Step Method

**Step 2.1 Business Review**
- Analyze 3–5 years of spend, supplier performance, cost trends, and delivery metrics.
- Identify top performers and laggards; find root causes of variance.

**Step 2.2 Supply Market Analysis**
- Use Porter's Five Forces, PEST, and commodity-specific models.
- Map supplier concentration, technology roadmaps, geographic risk, and substitution potential.
- Monitor competitors' supplier choices as intelligence.

**Step 2.3 Internal Analysis**
- Collect pain points from R&D, manufacturing, quality, and finance.
- Identify capability gaps between what the market offers and what the company needs.
- Align procurement goals with corporate strategic intent.

**Step 2.4 Set Objectives**
Define 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year targets:
- Cost / TCO reduction targets.
- Quality / yield improvement targets.
- Delivery / lead-time compression targets.
- Innovation / technology-access targets.
- Risk / diversification targets.

**Step 2.5 Formulate Strategy**
For each major category, decide:
- **Make vs Buy**: what stays in-house vs outsourced.
- **Supplier model**: OEM, ODM, JDM, EMS, turnkey, consignment, buy/sell.
- **Relationship depth**: transactional → preferred → strategic → exclusive/partnership.
- **Cost strategy**: target costing, VA/VE, design-to-cost, should-cost modeling.
- **Capacity & network**: where to locate supply, backup sources, and logistics paths.

**Step 2.6 Execution & Capability Plan**
- Break strategy into annual initiatives with owners and milestones.
- Build procurement team capabilities (commodity expertise, negotiation, finance, engineering).
- Use external advisors or benchmarking where internal knowledge is insufficient.

### Step 3 — Organize the Procurement Iron Triangle
For each major category, staff three roles:
- **CEG (Commodity Expert Group)**: owns strategy, supplier selection, and commercial terms.
- **TQC (Technical Quality Certification)**: owns technical qualification, quality standards, and early R&D engagement.
- **Procurement Execution**: owns PO placement, delivery follow-up, and supplier operational performance.

All three must agree before a supplier is elevated to strategic status.

### Step 4 — Supplier Tiering & Governance
Segment suppliers into four tiers and apply differentiated management:
- **Strategic**: joint roadmaps, executive sponsorship, long-term agreements, co-investment.
- **Preferred**: multi-year contracts, volume commitment, competitive pricing.
- **Qualified**: transactional, performance-monitored, annual re-evaluation.
- **Limited/Eliminate**: wind down with risk-managed exit plans.

### Step 5 — Ethics & Compliance Guardrails
- Enforce two-person rules for negotiations above threshold.
- Require conflict-of-interest declarations.
- New suppliers must pass integrity pledges and background checks.
- Rotating buyers out of categories to prevent cozy relationships.

## Key Principles

- **Procurement's mission is to help the company win, not just to buy cheap**: price is one variable in total value.
- **Strategic suppliers are an extension of R&D**: engage them at concept stage, not at mass-production stage.
- **Trust is built through reciprocity**: short-term squeezing destroys long-term resilience.
- **Category strategy without execution plan is a wishlist**: translate every strategy into annual initiatives with accountable owners.
